## 1. Brand platform

### The idea in the name

*Praxis* is theory put into practice: an idea made real by doing it. That is the whole business. Every institution has a picture of how it is meant to run: the policies, the organisation chart, the budget, the process map. And every institution has the way it really runs: numbers rebuilt by hand each month, approvals that live in one person’s memory, policies that live in a PDF. JBS Praxis closes the gap between the two.

### Purpose

Close the gap between how Africa’s institutions are meant to run and how they really run.

« Réduire l’écart entre la façon dont les institutions africaines doivent fonctionner et la façon dont elles fonctionnent réellement. »

### Essence

**Order, made visible.**

Most institutions run on effort: heroic month-ends, spreadsheets passed from hand to hand, decisions held in a few people’s heads. JBS Praxis does two things. First we make the order: we design how money, people, operations and risk should flow. Then we make it visible: we build the system that shows, every day, whether they do. Consulting makes the order; engineering makes it visible.

Two supporting ideas sit beneath the essence. They are not separate slogans; they are what order makes possible.

| Supporting idea | What it means | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Institutions in motion | Once leaders can see clearly, the institution moves: faster decisions, cleaner operations, growth that holds. | Case studies, the "before and after" of an engagement, the About page |
| Ask your company anything | How a well-run institution feels to lead: a question gets a straight answer, from the institution’s own data, in seconds. | The homepage line, the AI assistant, HQ overviews, product demos |

The homepage keeps the line chosen earlier, **"Ask your company anything."**, always followed by the support line that says how (section 14). "Order, made visible" is the internal compass and the brand’s closing line. It is used sparingly in public (brand films, the About page, the last page of a proposal), never as a repeated slogan.

**Consulting and engineering for Africa’s institutions.** « Conseil et ingénierie pour les institutions africaines. »

The descriptor tells a newcomer what JBS Praxis is. It sits with the logo in the website header area, on the LinkedIn profile, in email footers, on proposal covers and in every boilerplate.

### Narrative

Every institution has two versions of itself. One is on paper: the policies, the organisation chart, the budget, the process map. The other is how the work really gets done: numbers rebuilt by hand at month-end, approvals that wait for one person, decisions made without the facts. The distance between the two is where money leaks, people tire and boards lose confidence.

JBS Praxis exists to close that distance. Praxis means theory put into practice. We start by seeing how the institution really runs. We design how it should run. We build the system that runs it that way and shows, every day, that it does. Then we stay until it holds, and hand the keys to the institution’s own team.

The result is an institution that can answer for itself. Ask it anything, and it answers.

« Chaque institution existe en deux versions. L’une est sur le papier : les procédures, l’organigramme, le budget, la cartographie des processus. L’autre est la façon dont le travail se fait vraiment : des chiffres reconstitués à la main chaque fin de mois, des validations qui attendent une seule personne, des décisions prises sans les faits. C’est dans cet écart que l’argent se perd, que les équipes s’épuisent et que les conseils d’administration perdent confiance.

JBS Praxis existe pour réduire cet écart. Praxis signifie la théorie mise en pratique. Nous commençons par observer comment l’institution fonctionne réellement. Nous concevons la façon dont elle devrait fonctionner. Nous construisons le système qui la fait fonctionner ainsi et qui le montre, chaque jour. Puis nous restons jusqu’à ce que tout tienne, et nous remettons les clés aux équipes de l’institution.

Le résultat : une institution capable de rendre compte d’elle-même. Posez-lui toutes vos questions : elle répond. »

## 2. Brand architecture

JBS Praxis is a **masterbrand**: one name carries the reputation. Everything clients buy or use sits under it with a plain descriptive name. Only a product sold on its own gets its own name, and it is endorsed by the masterbrand.

```
JBS Praxis                                   masterbrand · jbspraxis.com
│   Descriptor: Consulting and engineering for Africa's institutions
│
├── Method        The Praxis Method: See, Design, Build, Hold
├── Offer         Free operations audit (the See stage), then an engagement
│
├── Practice areas (business domains)
│     Money · Human Capital · Operations · Risk and Governance · Data and AI
│
├── Industries
│     Pharmaceuticals · Consumer goods · Oil and gas · Public sector ·
│     Financial services and logistics
│
├── Platforms (plain names, always with the masterbrand)
│     ├── JBS Praxis HQ              staff workspace     hq.jbspraxis.com
│     └── JBS Praxis Client Portal   for clients         portal.jbspraxis.com
│
├── Insights      JBS Praxis Insights (articles; replaces "Kaizen Hub")
├── Tools         Cost of poor quality report · Cost of poor quality calculator
│
└── Products (own name, endorsed)
      └── nfelia                     "nfelia, a JBS Praxis product"
```

Naming rules:
- Always write **JBS Praxis** in full. Never "JBS" alone, never "Praxis" alone in client-facing words, never "JBSPraxis".
- "JBS" is not expanded in public. JBS Praxis is the name (owner to confirm; see Appendix C).
- The legal entity name appears only in legal footers, contracts and invoices. The brand name never changes when the legal entity is registered or renamed.
- **JBS Praxis HQ** on first mention, then **HQ**. **JBS Praxis Client Portal** on first mention, then **Client Portal**; in running text for clients, "your portal". In French: « le portail client JBS Praxis », then « votre portail client ».
- **A new offer gets its own name only if it is sold on its own to buyers who never buy consulting** (nfelia is the test case). Everything else takes a descriptive name under JBS Praxis.
- **JBS Praxis HQ is the system our own firm runs on.** It is shown as proof that we run on what we build (owner to confirm whether it will ever be sold).
- Modules inside HQ and the Client Portal use everyday nouns (section 5): Projects, Leads, Proposals, Invoices, Payments, Team, Time, Approvals, Reports, Settings.
- Practice pages carry a descriptive subtitle, for example "Money: finance systems, IFRS and OHADA reporting, cash and receivables".
- Client work built by JBS Praxis (for example MyCity App) is shown as proof, never as a JBS Praxis product.
- nfelia keeps its own identity. Next to JBS Praxis, use the endorsement line "a JBS Praxis product" in Plus Jakarta Sans at body-text size, never larger than the nfelia logo.
- **Partners and co-branding.** Work delivered with a partner firm names the partner only with both firms' approval ("delivered with Tales Consulting"). Co-branded proposals show both logos at equal height on the cover, separated by a 1px Line rule; JBS Praxis leads inside the document.
- **"intelligence@" is an address, never a displayed name.** The word "Intelligence" is no longer used as a brand or team name.

## 3. Personality

**The quiet expert, with an engineer’s rigour, who knows the terrain.**

Think of the most trusted senior partner you have worked with: unhurried, precise, generous with what they know, never needing to impress. Underneath, an engineer’s habit of showing the working: the number, the system, the reason. And they know the terrain: IFRS and OHADA, naira and CFA francs, regulator inspections, unreliable power and patchy connectivity. They design for it.

| Trait | Sounds like | Never sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Composed | "Here is what we found, and what we recommend." | "Huge news!!" |
| Precise | "Receivables fell 18% in Q3, mainly in two regions." (illustrative) | "Things improved significantly." |
| Generous | Explains the why, shares the method, gives the next step. | Withholds, gatekeeps, talks down. |
| Rigorous (the engineer’s touch) | Shows the data source, the assumption, the trade-off. | Hand-waves, oversimplifies until it is wrong. |
| Grounded | "We designed the approval flow to work on a weak connection and through a month-end in two currencies." | "Global best practice says…", imported templates |
| Discreet | Respects confidentiality; describes rather than names. | Name-drops, boasts about clients. |

What the brand is not: a hacker, a military command, a sci-fi console, a startup hype machine, a faceless bureaucracy.

## 4. Voice

### The four principles

1. **Plain.** Everyday business words. If a CFO would not say it in a meeting, we do not write it.
2. **Precise.** Numbers over adjectives, specific nouns over vague ones, one meaning per word.
3. **Warm.** Write to one person. Use "you" and "we". Give the next step. Never cold, never chummy.
4. **Measured.** The Big Four discipline: lead with the conclusion, support it with structure and evidence, recommend clearly. Formal enough for a board pack, never stiff.

### How to write

- **Answer first.** Put the conclusion or the action in the first sentence. Supporting detail follows.
- **Short sentences.** Aim for 15 to 20 words. One idea per sentence.
- **Active voice.** "We verified your payment", not "Your payment has been verified by our team".
- **Sentence case** for everything: headings, buttons, labels, email subjects. No all caps, no title case.
- **No exclamation marks** in client-facing writing. One is acceptable in an internal celebration message.
- **Numbers as numerals**, with units: "3 projects", "₦4.2m", "12 days". Spell out only at the start of a sentence.
- **British English** (organisation, programme, licence, centre, working day).
- **Contractions** (we’ll, don’t, couldn’t) are fine in interface text and transactional email. Write them out in proposals, reports, contracts and the About page.
- **Typographic marks** in published copy: single quotes first in English (‘ ’, with “ ” inside them), « » in French, and ’ for apostrophes in both languages.
- **No hype or pseudo-technical intensifiers**: revolutionary, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, synergy, leverage (as a verb), disrupt, seamless, robust, next-generation, game-changing, unlock, empower, supercharge, elite, premier, Tier-1, deterministic, forensic, immutable, hardcoded, cognitive, zero-defect, unbreakable, "Code as Law". Say what is true instead: "every change is logged" (not immutable), "built into the system" (not hardcoded), "rule-based" (not deterministic), "a detailed review" (not forensic). FR : « chaque modification est tracée », « intégré au système », « fondé sur des règles », « une revue détaillée ».
- **No guaranteed outcomes** (return on investment, savings, uptime) unless a signed contract commits to them. Write "we estimate", "in our assessment", "typically".
- **No metaphors from war, space, hacking or injury**: command, battlespace, strike, deploy (for people), node, matrix, terminal, protocol, transmission, clearance, kill switch, god mode, bleeding, haemorrhage, disease, graveyard.

### Numbers, money, dates

- **Currency in English**: ₦ before the amount, no space (₦1,250,000; ₦4.2m; ₦1.1bn). FCFA after the amount, with a space (2,500,000 FCFA; 2.5m FCFA).
- **Currency in French**: every currency after the amount (1 250 000 ₦ ; 2 500 000 FCFA). In tables « 4,2 M₦ », in sentences « 4,2 millions de nairas ». « Md » for milliard.
- **Dates and times**: Thursday 5 November 2026, 10:30 (WAT). FR : jeudi 5 novembre 2026, 10 h 30 (heure d’Afrique de l’Ouest, WAT). Lagos and Douala share WAT, so state the time zone once.
- **Periods**: Q3 2026 / T3 2026; FY2026 / exercice 2026.
- **Negative amounts**: a true minus sign (−, U+2212) in interfaces; brackets in financial statements.
- **Phone placeholders**: +234 800 000 0000 (Nigeria), +237 6 99 00 00 00 (Cameroon).
- **In code**, format numbers, money and dates with the intl formatters (`NumberFormatter`, `IntlDateFormatter`) for `en_GB` and `fr_FR`, never by hand. Check that the web PHP has the intl extension (the server’s command-line PHP does not).

### The formal touch (Big Four / McKinsey)

Used in proposals, reports, board-facing pages and the About page:
- State the situation, the complication and the recommendation, in that order.
- Headings in proposals, reports and slides are action titles: a full sentence that states the takeaway ("Receivables fell 18% in Q3, mainly in two regions", illustrative), not a topic ("Receivables").
- Every proposal opens with a one-page executive summary: the situation, what we recommend, what it changes, what it takes.
- Group points in threes where the content allows; never force it.
- Use "we recommend", "the evidence suggests", "in our assessment". Avoid "we think", "maybe", "kind of".
- Quantify the stakes: the cost of poor quality, the hours lost, the risk exposure.

### Tone by context

The voice stays the same; the tone adjusts to the moment.

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Confident, calm, inviting | "See how your organisation really runs. Book a free audit and demo." |
| Proposals and reports | Formal, structured, evidence-led | "We recommend consolidating the three ledgers into one, which removes 11 days from the month-end close." (illustrative) |
| Client emails | Warm, brief, one clear action | "Your account is ready. Choose a password to sign in." |
| HQ and Client Portal | Efficient, neutral, specific | "Invoice INV-0042 sent to Acme Ltd." |
| Errors and warnings | Calm, never blaming, always a fix | "We couldn’t save the invoice because the due date is missing. Add a due date and save again." |
| Internal alerts | Factual, urgent only when it is | "Acme Ltd uploaded proof of payment for INV-0042 (₦1.2m). Verify it in Payments." |
| AI assistant | A senior colleague who knows the numbers | "Receivables stand at ₦48.6m, up 6% on September. Two customers in Lagos account for most of the rise. Source: Invoices, updated today at 09:40." |

### The AI assistant

- Answer first, then the detail. Always name the data source and when it was last updated.
- Say plainly when it does not know: "I can’t answer that from your data yet. Invoices before March 2026 aren’t in HQ." / « Je ne peux pas encore répondre à partir de vos données : les factures antérieures à mars 2026 ne sont pas dans HQ. »
- Never invent a figure, a client or a person. Never name staff or leadership; refer to roles.
- Reply in the language of the question.

### French (full parity)

French is a first language of the brand, not a translation afterthought. Every public page, email and client-facing screen ships in English and French.

- **Vouvoiement** always. **Register**: *courant soigné*, the French of a Big Four letter: complete sentences, no familiar turns of phrase, no administrative stiffness (« veuillez agréer » only in formal letters).
- **Mood**: buttons, menu items and labels take the infinitive (« Réserver un audit offert », « Envoyer l’invitation »). Sentences addressed to the reader take the *vous* imperative (« Choisissez un mot de passe pour vous connecter. »).
- **Typography** uses Unicode characters, not the space bar. A narrow no-break space (U+202F) goes before the semicolon, question mark, exclamation mark and per cent sign, inside « », between a number and its unit, and as the thousands separator. A no-break space (U+00A0) goes before the colon. Decimals take a comma, and apostrophes are typographic (’).
- **Sentence case**: « Audit de diagnostic », never « Audit de Diagnostic ».
- **Allow for length**: French runs 20 to 30% longer. Buttons and labels are designed for the French length first, and layouts pass a pseudo-localisation test at +30% length.
- **Translate meaning, not words.** "Free audit" is « audit offert », never « audit libre » (libre means unrestricted).
- **Names**: JBS Praxis, JBS Praxis HQ and nfelia never change. Client Portal is translated as a name: « portail client JBS Praxis », then « votre portail client ».
- **No anglicisms where French has the word**: workflow → circuit de validation or processus ; dashboard → tableau de bord ; task force → équipe projet ; lead → prospect.
- **Inclusive writing**: no point médian (« collaborateur·rice »). In the interface use epicene nouns (« la personne », « l’équipe »); in recruitment copy use doublets (« collaborateurs et collaboratrices »).
- **Review**: a native francophone reviewer, ideally in Cameroon, signs off every French string before it ships (section 16).

## 6. Logo

The logo and its colours do not change. This section governs how it is used. The files are in the brand kit (`brand/kit/logo/`).

### The mark

Ten horizontal bars form a diamond around the JBS letters and the PRAXIS wordmark. Each bar is split down the centre: dark on the left, teal on the right. The ends are cut on the diagonal. The split, dark into teal, is the brand’s story in one shape: the institution as it runs today, and as it runs with the order made visible.

### Versions

| Version | Files | Use |
|---|---|---|

| Stacked, one colour | `stacked-ink.svg`, `stacked-white.svg` | One-colour print, stamps, embossing, watermarks |

The flat versions are built from the existing artwork: the same shapes, with the gradients replaced by the two flat colours they run between (Ink and Teal; white and Bright Teal on dark). The original gradients pass through the retired neon cyan, so the flat versions are the default everywhere. A clean vector redraw of the master (the current file is an automatic trace) is commissioned before print runs of more than 12mm height on paper.

### Rules

- **Clear space**: empty space on every side equal to the height of one bar plus one gap (about 5% of the stacked logo’s height; 25% of the monogram’s height).

- **The HQ app launch** is the one exception to "animate" and "separate the bars from the letters", approved by the owner on 30 October 2026. When the installed HQ app starts, the reversed stacked logo shows its letters first and the ten bars arrive from both sides to meet at the split (section 11, "The HQ launch moment"). Every other rule here still applies, and the moment ends on the logo exactly as drawn.

## 8. Typography

Two typefaces, clearly different jobs. **Newsreader speaks; Plus Jakarta Sans works.**

### Newsreader (serif, the voice)

A contemporary text serif designed for reading on screens, with optical sizes. It gives JBS Praxis the authority of a serious publication: the board paper, the annual report, the considered argument.

Use for: page headlines, section headlines, the homepage line, pull quotes, the large headline numbers on the website and in reports, email headlines, document titles.

- Weights: 400 for large display, 500 for headlines, 600 sparingly for small headlines. Italic for quotations only, not for emphasis inside a headline.
- Optical size: let the variable font’s `opsz` follow the size (`font-optical-sizing: auto`).
- Letter-spacing: -0.01em at 40px and above; 0 below.
- Numbers: `font-variant-numeric: lining-nums proportional-nums` when static, `lining-nums tabular-nums` while animating.
- Never for buttons, form labels, table text or navigation.

### Plus Jakarta Sans (sans, the work)

A warm, open geometric sans with excellent legibility at small sizes and tabular figures. It runs the product: everything a person reads to get something done.

Use for: body text, navigation, buttons, forms, tables, labels, HQ and Client Portal interfaces, captions, email body text.

- Weights: 400 body, 500 UI and emphasis, 600 labels and small headings inside the product, 700 rarely (a single key figure in a table).
- Numbers in tables, invoices and dashboards: tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`).
- Never set in all caps. Small labels stay in sentence case at 600 weight instead.

### Type scale (web)

A perfect fourth (1.333) for display sizes; 2px steps for product sizes. Sizes in px; rem in code (16px = 1rem).

| Role | Face | Size / line height | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display (homepage line, hero) | Newsreader | 64 / 72 (mobile 40 / 48) | 400 |
| H1 (page title) | Newsreader | 48 / 56 (mobile 34 / 42) | 500 |
| H2 (section) | Newsreader | 36 / 44 (mobile 28 / 36) | 500 |
| H3 | Newsreader | 26 / 34 (mobile 22 / 30) | 500 |
| Headline number (site, reports) | Newsreader | 56 / 60 (mobile 40 / 44) | 400 |
| Lead paragraph | Plus Jakarta Sans | 20 / 32 | 400 |
| Body | Plus Jakarta Sans | 16 / 26 | 400 |
| Small body, table text | Plus Jakarta Sans | 14 / 22 | 400 |
| UI label, button | Plus Jakarta Sans | 14 / 20 | 500 |
| Caption, helper text | Plus Jakarta Sans | 13 / 18 | 400 |
| Product page title (HQ, Client Portal) | Plus Jakarta Sans | 24 / 32 | 600 |
| Product section heading | Plus Jakarta Sans | 18 / 26 | 600 |

Rules:
- Line length 60 to 75 characters for reading text; serif paragraphs (rare) may run to 80 with 1.6 line height.
- One H1 per page. Never skip a level for size; change the style, not the structure.
- Left-aligned text. Centre only short display lines in a hero or an email headline. Never justify.
- Display and H1 use `text-wrap: balance`. Test every heading with the French string first.
- Figures that are compared (tables, invoices, KPIs) are tabular and right-aligned.

### Loading and fallbacks

- Self-host the latin and latin-ext subsets (the ₦ sign is in latin-ext). Newsreader with `opsz,wght` axes; Plus Jakarta Sans weights 400 to 700. Budget: no more than 150 KB of font files on a first visit, measured before PR2 ships.

## 9. Layout and structure

### The Praxis rule (signature device)

The signature comes from the logo, not from a layout grid: **horizontal rules only, never visible column lines.** It has three forms.

1. **Hairline**: 1px `#C5CCD6` on light, or 1px `#2E3549` on Ink, full content width. It separates sections in place of boxes.
2. **Bar stack**: for a section opener or a report cover. Three bars in the logo’s proportions (on screen, 4px bars with 4px gaps; on covers, 12px bars with 13px gaps), ends cut on the diagonal like the logo’s, dark (Ink or Petrol) left of the split and Teal right of it.
3. **The moment**: one 2px rule, Ink on the left and Teal on the right, with the split on the column where the answer starts. It draws left to right to join a question to its answer. Straight runs, at most one square turn, no dots, nodes or glows at the ends.

Use the rule as a device once or twice per page, never as a background pattern. In print: 0.25pt hairline, 1.2mm bars.

### Spacing

A 4px base with an 8-point rhythm. The allowed steps:

4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 and 128px

- Within components: 4 to 16. Between related elements: 16 to 24. Between sections: 64 to 128 on the website, 32 to 48 in HQ and the Client Portal.
- HQ and the Client Portal: a 240px navigation rail (Ink) and a fluid workspace on Mist.

### Responsive grid

| Width | Columns | Gutter | Side margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 375 (phone) | 4 | 16 | 16 |
| 768 (tablet) | 8 | 24 | 32 |
| 1024 (laptop) | 12 | 24 | 40 |
| 1280 and up | 12 | 24 | auto; content max 1200, reading text max 720 |

HQ tables on phones keep their first column fixed and scroll sideways inside the table, never the page.

### Shape and elevation

- **Website: no cards.** Content sits directly on full-width Mist or Paper bands, separated by Praxis rules. On the website, radius is 0 except controls (4px) and framed product screens (8px).
- **HQ and the Client Portal**: cards with an 8px radius and a 1px Line border; 4px for inputs, chips and buttons; 12px for dialogs.
- Elevation through borders first. Shadows only for things that float above the page (menus, dialogs, toasts): `0 8px 24px rgba(12, 20, 43, 0.12)`.
- No gradients as decoration. A single gradient is allowed inside data (an area chart fill fading from Teal to transparent).

### Iconography

- Lucide icon set, 1.5px stroke at every size (`absoluteStrokeWidth`), 20px default, 16px in dense tables, drawn in the text colour or Deep Teal for interactive icons.
- Icons accompany words; an icon alone needs a tooltip and an accessible name.
- Targets: at least 24px, and 44px on touch screens.

## 11. Motion

**Composed and precise, with living data.** The brand moves the way a well-run organisation does: things settle into place. Where data is involved, motion shows it arriving. Nothing bounces, spins or glows.

### Principles

1. **Settle, don’t perform.** Elements arrive and come to rest. No overshoot, no elastic easing.
2. **Motion explains data.** Lines draw as data arrives, values settle, charts grow from their baseline. Motion that does not explain something is cut.
3. **One orchestrated moment per page.** The website hero has one composed sequence. Sections below do not each animate in.
4. **Respond to people.** Opening, expanding, saving, confirming: motion that shows what changed is always welcome.
5. **Respect reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion`, ambient and data motion stop and scripts render the final state; only short opacity fades remain.

### Living-data devices (the "Institutions in motion" layer)

| Device | Where | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing line | Homepage hero, section openers, reports | The Praxis rule draws left to right to join a question to its answer; `--motion-story` (1200ms) with `--ease-settle`, once |
| Settling number | Headline numbers, KPI tiles | The final value is in the page from the start. A hidden visual layer rolls only the last two significant digits, from 97% of the value to the value, over `--motion-data` (600ms) with `--ease-settle`, once. Screen readers hear only the final value |
| Growing chart | Charts on first view | Bars and areas grow from the baseline over `--motion-data`; staggered 40ms per series at most |
| Quiet flow | One hero per site, optional | A very slow movement of fine horizontal lines (cycle 20s or longer, `--ease-linear`), marked `data-motion="ambient"`, paused when off-screen and stopped for reduced motion |
| Live tick | HQ dashboards with live data | A new value fades in over `--motion-quick`; a 1px Bright Teal marker pulses once on Ink |

### The hero moment (storyboard)

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0ms | The page is complete: headline, support line, button and a framed HQ screen (demo data) showing a question and its answer. |
| 0 to 150ms | The question’s text fades up on the framed screen (`--motion-fade`). |
| 150 to 1350ms | The Praxis rule draws from the question to the answer, Ink then Teal across the split (`--motion-story`, `--ease-settle`). |
| 1350 to 1950ms | The answer’s last two digits settle (`--motion-data`). |
| After | Stillness. Nothing loops. |

With reduced motion, the final frame shows at once.

### The HQ launch moment (storyboard)

The one place the logo moves (section 6): when the installed HQ app starts. It plays once per start of the app, never in a browser tab and never between pages. The owner chose it on 30 October 2026 ("B. Converge").

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| Before 0ms | iPhone and iPad show the startup image (section 14). It is the first frame below, so the hand-off can’t be seen. |
| 0ms | Ink. The letters (JBS and PRAXIS) centred, and "HQ" beneath in Plus Jakarta Sans 500 in the secondary text colour on Ink (`#BBBDC4`). No bars, no rule yet. |
| 120 to 1060ms | The ten bars arrive from both sides and meet at the split: the white halves from the left, the Bright Teal halves from the right, each from 16% of the logo’s width away, fading in as it comes. The two bars nearest the letters go first and each pair after them 75ms later, outwards; each bar takes 640ms with `--ease-settle`. |
| 820 to 1520ms | The Praxis rule draws beneath the logo, left to right, white then Bright Teal across the split (700ms, `--ease-settle`). |
| 1650 to 1870ms | The launch screen fades out (220ms, `--ease-exit`) and HQ is in view. If HQ’s page is still loading, the logo holds until it has loaded, at most 4s from the start. |
| After | Stillness. A tap, a click or Escape at any point goes straight to the fade. |

With reduced motion, the complete logo, the rule and "HQ" show still for 400ms, then fade out over `--motion-fade` (150ms).

The logo is drawn from its own shapes and "HQ" from the letter outlines, so the first frame never waits for a font. Source: `praxis_core/resources/views/components/pwa/launch.blade.php`.

### Tokens

| Token | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--motion-instant` | 100ms | Hover, press, toggles |
| `--motion-fade` | 150ms | Opacity-only fades (kept for reduced motion) |
| `--motion-quick` | 200ms | Menus, tooltips, small reveals, every exit |
| `--motion-standard` | 320ms | Panels, dialogs, page sections entering |
| `--motion-data` | 600ms | Charts, value changes |
| `--motion-story` | 1200ms | The one orchestrated moment |

| Easing token | Curve | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ease-settle` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` | Default for things arriving and moving, and the drawing line |
| `--ease-enter` | `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)` | Entering the screen |
| `--ease-exit` | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)` | Leaving the screen (with `--motion-quick`) |
| `--ease-linear` | `linear` | Quiet flow only |

Animate `opacity` and `transform` only (plus `stroke-dashoffset` for drawing lines). Never animate layout properties. Scripts check `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` and render the final state: lines drawn, numbers at their value, charts at full height.

### Retired motion

Typewriter text effects, the animated logo GIF, the animated slogan GIF, neon glows and pulsing borders, "matrix" boot sequences, numbers counting up from zero, and fade-and-rise on every card are removed.

## 12. Interface writing patterns

These apply across the website, HQ and the Client Portal, in English and French.

### Buttons and links

- Start with a verb and name the outcome: "Send invitation", "Verify payment", "Download report", « Envoyer l’invitation ».
- Never "Submit", "OK", "Proceed", "Click here".
- The same action keeps the same word through the flow: a "Publish" button produces a "Published" confirmation.
- Link text names its destination ("Read the Money practice"), never "click here" or "learn more" alone.

### Forms

- A visible label on every field; placeholders only for format hints ("e.g. +234 800 000 0000").
- Mark what is optional, not what is required.
- Validate when a person leaves a field; clear the error as soon as it is fixed.

### Errors

Say what happened, why if it helps, and what to do next. Never blame, never apologise theatrically, never show codes or capitals.

> "We couldn’t send the invitation because that email address already has an account. Ask the person to sign in, or use a different email address."
> « Nous n’avons pas pu envoyer l’invitation : cette adresse e-mail est déjà associée à un compte. Demandez à la personne de se connecter, ou utilisez une autre adresse. »

### Empty states

What this is, why it is empty, how to start.

> "No invoices yet. An invoice is created here when a proposal is signed, or you can create one now." Button: "Create invoice".
> « Aucune facture pour l’instant. Une facture est créée ici dès qu’une proposition est signée ; vous pouvez aussi en créer une maintenant. » Bouton : « Créer une facture ».

### Confirmations

Name the action and the consequence, without assuming anyone’s pronouns.

> "Suspend access for :name? They will be signed out and can’t sign in until you restore access." Buttons: "Suspend access" / "Keep access".
> « Suspendre l’accès de :name ? Cette personne sera déconnectée et ne pourra plus se connecter tant que vous n’aurez pas rétabli son accès. » Boutons : « Suspendre l’accès » / « Conserver l’accès ».

### Notifications (HQ)

The title says what happened; the body gives the detail and the place to act.

> "Proof of payment uploaded" / "Acme Ltd uploaded proof of a ₦1.2m payment for INV-0042. Verify it in Payments."
> « Justificatif de paiement déposé » / « Acme Ltd a déposé un justificatif de paiement de 1 200 000 ₦ pour la facture INV-0042. Vérifiez-le dans Paiements. »

### Status labels

Short, plain and stored per object, because French past participles agree with their noun. Never reuse a feminine label for a masculine object. Always shown with the functional colour and its tint.

| Object | Labels (English / Français) |
|---|---|
| Invoice / Facture (f.) | Draft / Brouillon; Sent / Envoyée; Paid / Payée; Overdue / En retard; On hold / En attente |
| Proposal / Proposition (f.) | Draft / Brouillon; Sent / Envoyée; Awaiting signature / En attente de signature; Signed / Signée; Declined / Refusée |
| Payment / Paiement (m.) | Awaiting verification / En attente de vérification; Verified / Vérifié; Not verified / Non vérifié |
| Account / Compte (m.) | Active / Actif; Suspended / Suspendu |
| Issue / Anomalie (f.) | Open / Ouverte; In progress / En cours; Resolved / Résolue |

### Alt text

Describe what the image shows and why it is there, in the page’s language: "Containers in rows at a port at first light" / « Conteneurs alignés dans un port au lever du jour ». Decorative rules and patterns take an empty alt.
